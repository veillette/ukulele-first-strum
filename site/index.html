Your first practice

Get comfortable

Sit upright and rest the body of the uke against your torso. With the neck in your fretting hand, look down at the strings. From closest to your face to closest to the floor, they’re G, C, E, A.

Tune the strings

Pluck one open string at a time. Turn its tuning peg a little, then pluck again. Start with G and work down toward A.

Open the tuner
Play a C chord

Put your ring finger on the third fret of the A string, just behind the fret wire. Leave the other three strings open and brush across all four.

See the C fingering
Keep a steady beat

Count “one, two, three, four” out loud. Brush from G to A with the back of your index fingernail on each number. Keep the motion small and your wrist relaxed.

How to tune a string
  1. Pluck the selected string without pressing a fret. Let it ring. Mute the other strings with your hand if they’re ringing too.
  2. Flat? Tighten slightly to raise the pitch. Sharp? Loosen slightly to lower it. Pluck again after each small turn.
  3. If tuning by ear, play the reference note, then pluck your string. Adjust until they sound the same.

The high G is higher in pitch than C and E. If your uke has a low G or baritone tuning, use the tuning specified for your instrument.
